A loop repeats a span of the loaded track until you exit it. Start one from the **Loop** key, reshape it by hand, and the deck remembers it for the next time you load the track. The **Loop** tab holds one panel:

```
Loop
‹ beats ›
IN   OUT
```

Loops work on the loaded track's playhead, so the panel needs a track. Anything that has to find a beat (an auto loop, or a boundary being snapped) also needs an analysed BPM.

## The Loop key

One press does whichever of these applies first:

| Situation | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| A loop is already active | Exits the loop; playback carries on from wherever the playhead is |
| A saved loop sits under the playhead | Recalls it: seeks to its in point, activates it, and plays |
| Nothing saved under the playhead, no active loop | Starts an auto loop at the current length, snapped to the nearest beat |

The key is lit while a loop is active. Exiting does not erase the loop: its band stays on the waveform in grey, and pressing **Loop** with the playhead inside that band recalls it.

## Loops are remembered automatically

You do not save loops by hand. Whenever a loop becomes active, the deck remembers it for the loaded track:

- an auto loop started with **Loop**;
- a manual loop completed with **IN** and **OUT**;
- a loop recalled from a saved band.

Up to **10** loops are remembered per track. The limit is independent of loop length: a 1-beat loop and a 32-beat loop both take one place. When an eleventh loop is remembered, the least recently updated auto-remembered loop is replaced.

Resizing an active loop with **‹** or **›** updates the remembered loop in place instead of creating another one.

Exit a loop and its grey band remains on the waveform. Press **Loop** while the playhead is inside any grey band to reloop it.

## Change the loop length

The chip between **‹** and **›** holds the loop length in beats: **1**, **2**, **4**, **8**, **16**, **32**.

| Key | Action | Disabled |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **‹** | Halve the length (tooltip "Halve loop length") | At **1** |
| **›** | Double the length (tooltip "Double loop length") | At **32** |

1. Press **‹** or **›** to step the length.
2. Watch the running loop: changing the length while a loop is active resizes it from the playhead and updates the remembered loop.

The length chip is a readout, not a save control.

## Mark a loop by hand

**IN** and **OUT** mark the region by hand, always at the playhead.

1. Press **IN** with no loop. A pending in-point drops, marked on the waveform with a flag.
2. Press **OUT**. The region completes from that pending point and is remembered automatically.

With a loop already active, **IN** and **OUT** move that region's ends instead of starting a new one.

The panel reports these mistakes rather than acting on a half-valid region:

- **OUT** with nothing pending and no active loop: "Set Loop In before Loop Out, or edit an active loop."
- **OUT** before the pending in-point: "Loop out must be after loop in."

## Quantize loop boundaries

Loop boundaries respect the deck's **Q** setting (on by default). With it on, hand-set in and out points snap to the beat grid. With it off they land exactly where the playhead is. An auto loop always snaps its start to the nearest beat, whichever way **Q** is set.

## Delete a remembered loop

Saved loops are per track, stored in the library database next to the cues. They show as grey blocks on both the overview strip and the scrolling waveform.

| Gesture | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Right-click / long-press a saved band | Opens the band menu with **Delete loop** |
| Shift+hover a saved band | Turns the band red and shows a trash glyph |
| Shift+click a saved band | Deletes it immediately |
| Shift+click **Loop** | Deletes the saved loop under the playhead |

Deleting the loop that is currently active exits it first, then removes it. Deleting a loop removes its band from the waveform.

## What is not remembered

Loop rolls that you hold on the performance pads are temporary: they are not saved to the track. Imported loops and loops saved by another tool are left untouched; automatic loop memory never overwrites or evicts them.

See [Loop Roll](/docs/users/performance-pads/loop-roll) for the held-roll pad mode. Loop points get snapped to the beat grid, so a wrong BPM or downbeat moves them; fix that in [Beat grid](/docs/users/decks/beat-grid).
