Mixar makes sound through a master bus and, optionally, a separate **preview bus** for headphones. Both live under **Settings → Audio**, whose description is *Engine output and buses*.

For every field on that screen with its range and default, see [Settings → Audio](/docs/users/settings/audio).

## Send audio to a device

1. Open **Settings → Audio → Master bus**.
2. Pick the output under **Device**. The first entry is **System default**, which follows your operating system's current default output.
3. Confirm the channel layout: **Channel mode** is either **Stereo pair** or **Mono (fold L+R)**, and **Left channel** / **Right channel** are 1-based channel numbers clamped to what the device reports. **Right channel** is disabled in Mono.
4. Press **Save**.

Saving restarts the engine whenever it is running, then reloads the tracks that were already on the decks. A restart also happens if you change **Resampler quality** or **Buffer size** in the **Engine** card, or a bus **Sample rate**. Closing the page with unsaved edits asks **Unsaved settings** / *Save changes before closing?*, so you can Save, Discard, or Cancel.

Each bus has its own **Sample rate**. **Device default** follows the rate the device itself mixes at, which is what a fresh install uses. Buses on different devices may run at different rates; the engine graph runs at the master rate and the preview stream is resampled, so both stay aligned. Two buses on the same device share one stream and therefore one rate.

If the settings were written but the engine refused them, the toast reads **Settings saved, but not applied** and carries the error. **Save failed** means the file on disk could not be written at all.

The **System default** entry reports two channels, so the master bus defaults to channels 1 and 2.

## Route the headphones

Cueing needs its own output path, so it is off until you enable it:

1. Open **Settings → Audio → Preview bus**.
2. Turn on **Enable preview bus**.
3. Pick the **Device** and its channels. The defaults are the system default device on channels **3 and 4**, which assumes a four-channel interface where channels 1 and 2 carry master.
4. Press **Save**.

The channel numbers are clamped when you switch devices. If a stereo pair would collide on a device with fewer channels, Mixar moves the right channel over by one; on a single-channel device it switches to Mono.

The mixer's **Levels** popover shows an **Enable** button on its **Headphone** card while the preview bus is off. Pressing it opens Settings on the preview bus card so you can turn it on. See [Cueing](/docs/users/mixer/cueing).

## When nothing is audible

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| The app does not start | No output device was enumerated. The engine reports **No output device available** and startup aborts. Enable an output and relaunch. |
| Deck levels move, no sound | Master bus points at the wrong device, or at a channel pair the device does not drive. Recheck **Device** and **Channel mode**. |
| Only one side is audible | **Channel mode** is set to **Mono (fold L+R)**, which folds both sides into one channel by design. |
| Cueing is silent, master is fine | **Enable preview bus** is off, or the preview bus sits on a channel pair the interface does not own. |
| Clicks, dropouts, or a refusal to start | **Buffer size** is not a multiple of 64 frames. The hint states the rule: *Must be a multiple of 64 frames (mixer graph chunk size).* |

**Low latency** is per bus: where the driver reports a buffer-size range, Mixar requests the device's *smallest* callback period for that bus's stream (the engine still renders its own mixer chunk); a driver that reports no range keeps the configured size. Buses that share a device share the request. The **Buffer size** stays global. It is the mixer chunk the engine renders in one go, and it also sets the callback size Mixar requests from the backend, which may clamp it to what the device supports. It is not a per-device setting, because the callback period the driver actually runs at can differ and the engine absorbs that with a per-device buffer. Lower it on a solid driver for a snappier response, and raise it if you hear dropouts.
