Every deck has a grid of **eight performance pads**. The pads are not fixed-function buttons — the **mode tabs** above the grid decide what each pad does, so the same eight pads become hot cues, loop rolls, beat jumps, samples, stem controls, or pitch controls.

## The grid

- **Eight pads** in two rows: pads **1–4** on top, pads **5–8** on the bottom.
- **Mode tabs** sit directly above the grid. Selecting a tab switches what the pads do; each deck keeps its own selected mode.
- **Cue** is the default mode when a deck starts.
- The pads need a **track loaded** on that deck. Until then they are disabled, though you can still change the selected mode.
- **Stems** pads additionally need stems for the loaded track — either an [NI Stem file](/docs/users/performance-pads/stems#where-stems-come-from), or generated first.

## Pad modes

| Mode | What the pads do | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cue** | Hot cues — save, jump to, and delete marker points | [Hot Cues](/docs/users/performance-pads/hot-cues) |
| **Roll** | Temporary loops that play while the pad is held | [Loop Roll](/docs/users/performance-pads/loop-roll) |
| **Jump** | Move the playhead forward or back by a set number of beats | [Beat Jump](/docs/users/performance-pads/beat-jump) |
| **Sample** | Trigger samples from a bank of up to eight | [Sampler](/docs/users/performance-pads/sampler) |
| **Stems** | Mute or isolate the drums, bass, other, and vocals layers | [Stems](/docs/users/performance-pads/stems) |
| **Keys** | Play the root hot cue at a chosen pitch | [Keyboard](/docs/users/performance-pads/keyboard) |
| **Shift** | Shift the pitch of the whole deck up or down | [Key Shift](/docs/users/performance-pads/key-shift) |

Two pad modes found in other DJ software are **not available yet**: **Slicer** and **Stems FX**.

## Modifier keys

Several pad actions use **Shift** while pressing a pad — deleting hot cues and samples, and switching semitone pages in Keys and Shift. Hold **Shift** as you press, exactly as you would with a controller's shift bank.

## Beat-based modes and the grid

**Roll** and **Jump** move the playhead relative to the track's beat grid, so they need a track **BPM** to have been analysed. With **Quantize** enabled, a Roll starts and a Jump lands on the beat instead of wherever the playhead happened to be.
